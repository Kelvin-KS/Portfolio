Process

Tools have roles. I'm the first and last checkpoint.

I use AI heavily, and deliberately. Each tool has one job, and nothing ships until I've tested it myself.

  1. Me

    Understand

    Requirements, the client's vision, and what "done" means.

  2. Me

    Validate the flow

    Check mockups before design moves on: does the journey make sense, and are the key states covered?

  3. AI · ChatGPT

    Structure

    Turn my notes into a precise, complete prompt.

  4. AI · Claude

    Build

    Generate the design and implementation.

  5. AI · Grok

    Cross-check

    A second opinion on prompts, test cases and answers.

  6. Me

    Test & judge

    Real devices, DevTools and test cases. Accept, redirect or reject.

  7. Me

    Validate

    Retest until it meets the requirement and the client's vision.

Test & judge → Build repeats until it passes.

What I catch that AI misses

Problems I've found in AI-generated work, and the testing that catches each one.

  • Pages that 404 on refresh, links pointing to the wrong URLNavigation & link testing
  • Layouts that break on phones and tabletsResponsive testing on real devices
  • Console errors hidden behind a page that looks fineDevTools console & network checks
  • A fix in one place that breaks something elseRegression testing
  • Instructions ignored, or changes nobody asked forChecking results against requirements
  • Slow pages and heavy imagesLighthouse & performance audits
  • Designs that look like every other websiteDesign review & visual judgement

How formal?

Client deliverables get structured test cases and written reports. My own prototypes get exploratory testing. I choose the level of formality based on the risk.

Admin settingsRequirement not met
Requirement
Each admin gets clearly defined responsibilities, so a secondary admin can't be given more access than they need.
Observed
A generic page of on/off checkboxes. No defined roles and no limits on secondary admins.
Action
Wrote a more detailed specification for the AI, supported with moodboards, until the intent was clear.
Retest
Admin responsibilities defined per role, as required. Pass

Why it matters: giving each admin only the access they need is the principle of least privilege, a basic of good security.
